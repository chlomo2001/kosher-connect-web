### The £90

## 6. The rental lifecycle

`b95294b`, `9e64893`

His ask: reserved/booked → fetched → active → non-active → returned, every stage
on the customer's card, and no "stuck in a pool" flag while the phone is merely
fetched and not yet flown — raise it 24 hours before travel instead, colour plus
task.

`lib/rentalStage.mjs`, mirrored into `main.js` as `KC_STAGE`. Two stages were
missing at the two ends:

**`fetched`** — collected, not travelling yet. `getComputedStatus` had no room
for it (`r.toDate < today ? 'overdue' : 'active'`), so a phone collected a
fortnight early read Active and everything watching active rentals began asking
why its line was not live. A pool that has not been activated **is not a problem
on a phone sitting on somebody's kitchen table**, and a task saying it is trains
people to ignore tasks.

**`ended`** — asked what "non active" meant, he said: *"the person is back, but
hasnt retuned the phone/sim, so the line doesnt have to be actively running, but
still its not available yet until physically back"*. Three claims, three
behaviours: readiness is not chased, no red and no task, and the handset stays
counted as out. It hardens into a real Overdue after
`rental_return_grace_days` (Settings, default 3; set it to 0 for the old
behaviour).

`readinessDue` stays **true** for a genuinely overdue hire on purpose — a phone
still out well past its dates is either late or on a trip that got extended, and
if it is the second the customer is abroad on a line worth watching.

`OUT_WITH_CUSTOMER` exists because five screens asked "is this handset with a
customer" by pasting the same two-name list. The one that would have failed
silently is the rentals row renderer, where an unhandled stage fell through to a
"Returned" badge.

## 8. Two money findings, on the same customer

`ac5f94a`

The £90 was posted (ledger `#1592` £28.80, `#1593` £61.20, each naming what it
corrects) plus `amountPaid` on the two rental rows — the ledger is the books but
the Rentals screen reads the rental. **Balance £0.00**: £368.40 charged,
£368.40 paid.

Chasing why those rows had no `method` turned up something bigger: **no rental
has ever recorded how it was paid.** The form has asked cash / card / transfer
since the beginning; both save paths read the answer to decide *whether* money
moved and then dropped it. Repairs and bookings both carry theirs.

That is not cosmetic. `cashExpected()` counts only rows whose method is `cash`,
so a hire paid in cash never reached expected cash — **the till read over at the
end of every day** and the variance pointed at nothing anybody could name. The
manual has claimed cash-up shows "what came in by method" the whole time; now it
does.

Whitelisted against the `payment_method` enum on the way out: `method` is a
Postgres enum, so an unknown string fails the insert and takes the save with it.
A rental that will not save at the counter is worse than an unknown tender.

**Not backfilled** — 13 historical rental payments have no method and there is
no honest way to guess.

## 9. The contact tools, and reading his converters

`9465994`, `a60f96a`

The Contact Tools card listed five office-PC converters. Three had already been
rebuilt in-app — `pages/tools/contacts.js` says so in its own header — and the
in-app rows had been added on top without removing the rows they superseded.

Then he sent the actual tools off the PC. **They are HTML pages, not `.exe`**,
so the logic was readable, and comparing them settled everything:

| His tool | Finding |
| --- | --- |
| `Nokia_IB_to_VCF` | **Identical** to `parseNokiaIb` — record start `0x244`, stride 592, 1246 records, names `0x60`/`0xb4`, phones `0x1e`/`0x34`, same nibble decode, same `0x11` → `+` |
| `Nokia_C2_to_FIG` | Emits **exactly** our `FIG_KEYS`, down to `creator: com.figmessenger` and `sub_id: "1"`. Our FIG output is confirmed against the tool he actually restores phones with |
| `NEW_GOOD_XML_to_FIG` | Differences both ways — below |

His XML matcher is `/<sms\s+([^>]+?)\s*\/>/g`. `[^>]` stops at the first `>`, so
a message reading "u there? ->" never matches and is **dropped in silence**.
Ours already handled that: on a three-message file, his 2, ours 3.

But he never checked for an `<smses>` root and we demanded one, so a file his
tool ate happily was refused here — a job going back to the PC over a wrapper
element nobody reads. The messages are the test now, not the wrapper.

And a flaw that was **ours alone**, the quiet kind. Both attribute matchers read
only double-quoted values — but his *skips* a tag it cannot read
(`if (attrs.address || attrs.body)`) while ours pushed the record regardless. So
a single-quoted export did not fail and did not lose messages: it produced the
right **number** of entirely **blank** ones, into the zip as empty texts. An
error is recoverable and a missing message is noticeable; a backup full of blanks
is neither. Both quote styles now, and his guard adopted.
