## 1. Sub-views, and the two toolbars that were the same mistake twice

`ce8668e` · tasks #51, #53, #63, #64

**Sub-views.** A pill switcher under the tab bar, from the owner's tenancydesk
reference. Rentals has three: Rentals · Phone inventory · Availability, the

This **retired the side-by-side split**, which was the wrong answer to a right
complaint ("active rental and phone inventory can be side by side instead? it

cell had no hover rule at all, and lightening a filled swatch reads as switching
it off while darkening reads as pressing it.

**The two toolbars** were one mistake made twice: controls riding on the right
edge of a section heading. Fine for two, broken at six — they wrap, one drops to
a second line, and the heading is left with a screen of dead space beside it.
`.kc-toolbar` is now shared, so the next list gets the arrangement rather than a
third variation of it.

## 4. Due back the day after, a week before anybody is charged

`1a9bd38` · task #58 · **migration**

Owner: "change rental returns rule — due date is always a day after arrival —
end of rental, (and come up as task, amber bla bla) but late fees only once past
7 days."

One number carries both halves so there is no second setting to drift:
`rental_return_grace_days` moves 3 → 7 and now means *days past the return date
before a hire is chased **and** charged*.

| | |
|---|---|
| the day after `end_date` | **Due back.** Amber, a RETURNDUE task. Nothing charged. |
| after seven days | **Overdue.** Red, and the late fee starts counting from there. |

The money part is the one to check: `calcLateFeeDays` and `mgComputeLateFee`
both counted from `toDate + 1`, so a customer three days late was billed for
three days. They now count from `lateFeeFrom()`. Both were changed together on
purpose — one quotes the fee in the dialog, the other paints it on the row, and
the two disagreeing is the class of bug that cost £90 a fortnight ago.

New tests pin the thing the change is about: **due-back and first-chargeable-day
are different days**, so a phone can be late and free at the same time.

## 5. Carrier mail: pairing teaches the app which line owns the tag

## 6. An example number should not read like a number on file

`d60fd4c` · task #67

Owner: "the autofill fake data (like 07955123123) that the app generates to show
an example of what should be filled in, is same darkness as real ones."

It was. Placeholders were painted in `--muted`, `#4a4d50` at **8.37:1** on a
card — a value tuned for AAA *body ink*. At that weight an example sits at the
same visual level as a saved number and the eye reads the field as filled.

New `--placeholder` token, one step lighter per theme: `#666b72` on light
(5.28:1 on `--surface`, 4.85 on `--bg`, 4.52 on `--surface2`) and `#868e98` on
dark. All clear **WCAG 1.4.3 (AA)** on every surface a field can sit on, and
deliberately do **not** reach the AAA 7:1 the rest of the ink holds: the
`<label>` carries the meaning, and a hint that looks like content is the
accessibility problem being fixed, not the contrast.

Applied to every `input`/`textarea` rather than `.form-input` alone, since the
quick-add fields and search boxes show examples too. The Stripe card element
reads the same token.

## 9. Messages can start a text, not only answer one

`cf29ea5` · task #66

Owner: "should we add at messages a simple option of just sending an SMS to a
customer. e.g., compose, to (customer dropdown or free typed uk number), send?"

Everything on that screen began with *them*. You could reply to a text; you
could not open one — and the shop's own case is the other way round most of the
time (the phone is ready, the passport is back, the part has come in), so each
of those went out through WhatsApp on somebody's own handset where the business
has no record of it.

### A stated rule was widened, and it is worth knowing

`/api/sms` would previously never accept a destination from the browser — a
customer id or an inbound log row, never a number. That is what stopped it being
a way to text anybody from a page. **No server-side resolution can produce a
number the database has never seen**, so the rule was replaced by a narrower one
rather than dropped:

- **UK mobiles only** — `+447` and nine digits, refused in the browser and again
  on the server. A landline, an 09, a foreign number: all refused with the
  reason, pointed at the customer's own card.
- staff session as before
- logged in `email_log` like every other send

The counter shows the character and text count live, says what is stopping a
send *before* the press, and the confirm names the person or the number out
loud: the mistake worth catching is the right message to the wrong recipient.

Two things fell out of building it. `normalisePhoneE164` is now **mirrored** into
`main.js` beside `phoneProblem`, so the browser's idea of what a number becomes
cannot drift from the server's. And `[hidden]` is now honoured globally — it is
only a UA `display:none`, so `.form-group`'s own `display:flex` beat it and a
hidden field group stayed fully on screen.

## 10. Suggested replies, led by the record rather than the words

`be95f28` · task #55

The obvious build is a keyword matcher over what the customer wrote. **Production
said it would fire on almost nothing.** Every inbound text the shop has ever had,
read on 28 Aug:

```
"?"   "Hello"   "K"   "Ok.."   one referral spam, sent twice
"Hi there, any qlyx phones available?"
```

Five of seven carry nothing answerable. So `lib/smsSuggest.mjs` is **context-led
and word-steered**: what a person at the counter would say to somebody who texts
"?" comes from that person's record — the phone due back, the balance open, the
repair on the shelf, the flight on Tuesday — and the words are read on top of
that when they say something.

Nothing is offered whose facts are missing: no rate on file means no priced
answer, and a repair that is not finished gets "let me check", never "it is
ready".

**Chips, not a pre-fill.** A draft already sitting in the reply field is a draft
somebody sends without reading it, and these are guesses.

One number carrying two customer records (a duplicate, or a family sharing a
handset) **pools the facts and drops the first name**. "A phone on this number is
overdue" still holds for whoever answers; greeting the wrong brother by name does
not.

A fixture bug surfaced on the way: the harness seed claimed an inbound text was
from Yossi Taitelbaum while carrying a number belonging to nobody in the same
file, so the context path could never run in the harness at all.

## 11. A tab that is behind can no longer reverse a recorded payment

`cef37b2` · task #48 · **migration**

**The £90, explained.** Teitelbaum's ledger read −£90 after the rental had been
corrected to £61.20 received. It was fixed by hand. **It came back** — and the
second time was the tell, because nothing was re-typing it. A tab was
re-sending it.

A rentals save posts the *whole array*, so a tab left open on a phone at the
back of the shop holds a complete copy of every rental as it stood when that tab
last loaded. Correct a payment at the counter, then let the old tab save
anything at all — a note, a return, a new hire — and its stale `amountPaid` goes
back over the corrected one. `syncRentals`' ledger true-up then does its job
faithfully: target minus posted, post the difference, and £28.80 of money the
shop actually received is reversed by a `rental_adjustment` nobody asked for.

Nothing could tell that apart from an edit, because **a rental had no version** —
`created_at` only says when it was written down. `rentals.updated_at` is now
bumped by a trigger, handed to the client as `_rev` beside the blob (the shape
`listSims` already uses), and a **money** change arriving from a copy older than
what is stored is refused.

The narrowness is the design, and the case it must *not* block matters as much
as the one it must:

| the incoming copy | what happens |
|---|---|
| current, or a new rental | **saves** — corrections still work, including a payment corrected *downwards*, which is why the rule is not "payments never go down" |
| behind, but not touching money | **saves** — on a whole-array save a tab is behind for nearly every rental it did not itself edit |
| behind **and** the money differs | stored money kept, the rest of the row saved, and it is reported |

Compared field by field, not on a total: a payment up and a price down by the
same amount is two wrong ledger entries, not none. The tender is kept with the
payment it describes, or cash-up would count the shop's cash as a card.

Refused, the tab is **told** rather than left holding figures the database does
not have. Every save now carries the new versions back so a tab stops being
stale about its own writes.
