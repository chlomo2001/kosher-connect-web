## What is being done overnight, in order

### A1 — the robots.txt / noindex trap · confirmed present

KC's `public/robots.txt` today ends with `Disallow: /` after allowing eight
public paths. Only ONE page in the whole app carries a noindex meta tag
(`pages/manual.js`, added this afternoon). That is precisely the trap: the staff
app and the customer portal are *forbidden to fetch* and carry *no instruction
not to index*, so a URL picked up from anywhere else can be listed, and the
instruction that would remove it lives in a page the crawler may not read.

- Invert: `Allow: /`, and a `noindex, nofollow` meta on every non-public route.
- KC's non-public set: the staff shell (`components/AppShell.js`, which covers
  `/` and every `/[tab]`), the four `/tools/*` pages, `/portal`, `/manual`.
- Public and staying public: `/welcome`, `/phone-guide`, `/repair`, `/terms`,
  `/privacy`, `/refund`, `/login`.
- `Disallow` kept only where FETCHING is the harm: `/api/`, and any tokened
  customer link. KC's tokened surfaces to check: payment links, document
  download links (`/api/documents/download?id=`), and the portal's shape.
- Each remaining `Disallow` gets its sentence in the file.

### A2 — normalisers · KC already has three, and that is the finding

The brief says take the four functions as-is. KC cannot, and should not: it
already carries **three overlapping email normalisers and two phone ones**.

| exists | where | does |
|---|---|---|
| `normalizeEmail` | `lib/mappers.js` | lowercase/trim |
| `mailboxKey` | `lib/simMailMatch.mjs` | Gmail dots + `+tag` KEPT (deliberately — a tag names a SIM) |
| `normalizeUkNumber` / `phoneKey` | `lib/ukPhone.mjs` | UK display form and a last-10 key |

So the work is a `lib/identity.mjs` that states which key means what, and
adopting it where it genuinely fits — NOT a fourth copy. Two KC-specific
decisions:

- **Last 9 or last 10?** The brief says last 9. KC's `phoneKey` uses last 10 and
  the whole SIM-mail matcher is built on it. Last 10 keeps `07…` intact and is
  right here; the plan keeps 10 and writes down why.
- **`+tag` must NOT be stripped everywhere.** The brief strips it for dedupe;
  KC's carrier-mail matching depends on the tag naming one SIM. Two keys, named
  for what they are: `personKey` (tag dropped, for "is this the same person")
  and `mailboxKey` (tag kept, for "which SIM is this"). The bug this prevents is
  real and would be silent.
- `nameKey` is new and lands well: transliterated Hebrew names vary in order and
  spelling constantly, and sorted-token matching handles a real share of that.
- `findDuplicates` shaping is taken; the in-memory scan is NOT. KC has 797
  customers today and a duplicate scan already exists
  (`pages/api/customers/not-duplicate`, the 👥 Duplicates tool) — the keys want
  to become indexed columns, noted for a later migration.

`supabase/restore-test/` with `compare.sh` (18 aspects, grants and per-row md5

### B1 — the clarity scan · document only

`docs/clarity-scan.md`, seven lenses, one root cause, the spine sentence, Tier 1
renames with `file:line`, Tier 2 with both arguments, and a refuter pass that
actually kills findings. **No behaviour changes overnight.**

The root-cause hypothesis to test (theirs was "the system has no person in it"):
KC's candidate is **"the plan has no shape"** — a SIM's number, email, renewal
date and the customer's NAME all live inside a `legacy_extras` jsonb blob on
`sims`, denormalised per row. `customerName` is copied onto every SIM, so a
customer renamed in one place is stale in another; a renewal date is a string in
a blob rather than a column anything can index or a constraint can defend. Every
"which SIM is this?" problem in the carrier queue is downstream of it. The scan
will confirm or kill this — it is a hypothesis, not a conclusion.

### B3 — verify every claim · document plus safe rewording

`docs/claims-audit.md`: every user-facing statement that says the system will
create, change, send, verify, delete or record something; the table it implies;
whether a write exists. KC's highest-risk surfaces, in order:

1. **Money screens.** Anything claiming an action is recorded against a named
   person — the wallet, the till, cash-up. A false claim about money being
   recorded is worse in a shop than a false claim about a viewing.
2. **The 20 guides and the manual**, which is 29 screens of claims written today
   and has never been walked against the running app. Their audit found three
   guides describing controls that do not exist; ours has had no such pass.
3. **The next-action rows** — not built yet, so this is a rule for B2 rather
   than an audit target.

Rewording a false claim is a copy change and ships overnight. Building a missing
capability does not.
